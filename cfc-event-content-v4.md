## Money is the most honest conversation in international education.

Financial aid officers, admissions teams, and scholarship coordinators from participating institutions are at this fair to have it directly.

---

### The full cost

A budget built in rupees today will shift against the dollar, pound, or Australian dollar over a multi-year program.

- Tuition fees
- Accommodation and meals
- Health insurance
- Visa and enrollment fees
- Travel, initial and recurring
- Books and course materials

Admissions representatives at this fair can provide cost breakdowns specific to their institution, city, and program — not estimates from a website.

---

### Proof of funds and education loans

Most countries require liquid funds held in a bank account for a minimum period before the visa application date. An education loan sanction letter from a recognised Indian bank is accepted in most countries as a substitute or supplement.

Financial aid officers at this fair can confirm what their institution and destination country specifically requires, and whether a loan sanction letter applies in each case.

---

### Scholarships

| Merit-based | Need-based |
|---|---|
| Academic record, test scores, extracurricular history | Documented family financial position |
| Thresholds vary by institution and country | Amounts not always publicly listed |

Scholarship coordinators at this fair can confirm eligibility criteria and available aid for their specific programs. Families can have this conversation directly, before committing to an application.

---

### Online programs

Tuition is typically 30 to 60 percent lower than on-campus. Visa-related financial requirements do not apply. Representatives from online programs at this fair can walk through the actual cost structure and payment requirements.

---

Parents and guardians are the decision makers on loans, funds, and financial risk. This fair is designed for that conversation too.

## A visa is issued to a prepared applicant.

Counsellors and global outreach teams at this fair work through visa timelines and documentation requirements for every destination country represented.

---

### The timeline

| Stage | Time needed |
|---|---|
| Passport (new or renewal) | 4 to 8 weeks |
| English proficiency test | 6 to 12 weeks |
| Financial documentation | 4 to 8 weeks |
| University application and decision | 4 to 16 weeks |
| Visa application after offer | 3 to 8 weeks |

Starting three months before the intended intake is already late. Counsellors at this fair help students identify where they are in this timeline and what moves first.

---

### Documents every application requires

- Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity; some countries require 12)
- Final offer letter from the university
- Proof of financial ability or education loan sanction letter
- English proficiency score
- Academic transcripts, attested where required

---

### Country-specific requirements

Visa requirements are set by the destination country, not the university. Financial thresholds, document formats, and processing times differ across the US, UK, Australia, Canada, and Europe. Global outreach teams from each institution at this fair can speak to what their specific country requires.

---

### Language requirements beyond the language of instruction

A program taught in English does not automatically waive the host country's language requirement. Germany, France, and several other countries have language requirements tied to visa status. Students planning for non-English-speaking countries can get this clarified directly at the fair.

## Colleges have requirements. Here is what they actually mean.

Admissions teams at this fair go beyond what is published on a university website. Bring your academic record and your questions.

---

### Grade conversion

Indian mark sheets require credential evaluation for most international applications. WES is the standard in North America. UK ENIC applies in the United Kingdom.

| Indian percentage (approximate) | US GPA equivalent |
|---|---|
| 90 and above | 4.0 |
| 80 to 89 | 3.5 to 3.9 |
| 70 to 79 | 3.0 to 3.4 |
| 60 to 69 | 2.5 to 2.9 |
| Below 60 | Below 2.5 |

Minimum requirements vary by program. Admissions officers at this fair can tell you where your profile stands for specific programs.

---

### English proficiency tests

| Test | Where accepted |
|---|---|
| IELTS Academic | UK, Australia, Canada, Europe |
| TOEFL iBT | USA, Canada, globally |
| PTE Academic | Australia, UK, New Zealand |
| Duolingo English Test | Select universities globally |

Score requirements differ by program level. Confirm the minimum for each specific program directly with the admissions team at this fair.

---

### Standard documents

- Academic transcripts (all years, attested where required)
- Recommendation letters (2 to 3, from teachers or academic supervisors)
- Statement of purpose
- Standardised test scores where applicable (SAT, ACT, GRE, GMAT)
- Financial documentation

Admissions representatives can confirm exactly what their institution requires and in what format.

---

### Holistic admissions in practice

International universities read applications. Academic record, statement of purpose, and recommendation letters are all evaluated together. A drop in grades, a gap year, or a program change will be noticed. Professors and admissions officers at this fair can explain what a competitive application looks like for their specific program.

## The fair works better when students come ready.

This is not a general information session. Admissions officers, professors, scholarship coordinators, and counsellors are here to have specific conversations. The more prepared a student is, the more they take away.

---

**Know the program, not just the college name.**
Representatives answer specific questions. A student who has read the program structure gets a different conversation than one who has not.

**Rankings measure research output, not fit.**
Program structure, faculty research areas, placement data, and campus culture matter more for most students than a global ranking. Ask representatives about outcomes, not reputation.

**Admissions is not an entrance exam.**
There is no single score that determines admission. Statements of purpose are read. Recommendation letters are contacted. Inconsistencies in academic record are noticed and asked about. Professors and admissions officers at this fair can walk students through what a strong application looks like.

**Parents make the final decision in most Indian families.**
Representatives know this. Bring them.

## Online Programs: A foreign degree does not always require leaving India.

Institutions at this fair offering online degrees are present to answer questions that a program page cannot.

---

### Cost

Online tuition is typically 30 to 60 percent lower than on-campus tuition for the same program. Living costs, travel, and visa fees do not apply.

---

### Three questions worth asking representatives directly

**Is the degree UGC-recognised?**
Degrees from institutions not on the UGC's recognised foreign universities list are not accepted for higher education admission or government employment in India. Representatives can confirm recognition status.

**How have alumni fared in the Indian job market?**
UGC recognition is the baseline. What happens after graduation matters. Alumni outcomes in India are a fair question for any representative at this event.

**What does the payment process look like?**
Tuition is billed in foreign currency. Not all Indian bank accounts support international transactions by default. Representatives can walk through exactly what is needed and how long it takes to set up.

---

### Schedule compatibility

Live sessions and some assessments may be scheduled in the institution's home time zone. Ask the representative for the live session schedule before making a decision.
